## 2. Clique Structure

Six groups emerged from the page 30 circle diagram. They overlap — several girls sit in multiple circles.

### A. The Core Four (Bell)
**Mia — Natalia — Olivia N — Ada**

The dominant clique. Originally Mia/Natalia/Olivia N/Alicia, until Ada joined and Alicia was pushed out. A tight Ada–Mia–Natalia sub-clique has formed within it, which has "gotten mixed reactions" from others. This is the group most girls in Bell homeroom orient toward, whether as members, aspirants, or exiles.

### B. The Chinese Girls / "Clover Cats" (cross-homeroom)
**Ashley — Mia — Olivia Q — Milan — Ellie**

Ashley's primary social world. Olivia Q (Bareres), Milan (Gryser), and Ellie (Gryser) are the "random Chinese girls" — Ashley's #1 picks. Mia bridges into this group from the Core Four. The group has **chilly relations** (star notation on diagram) with the Indian girls but no active dispute.

### C. Alicia's Cross-Group (Bell + Babu)
**Alicia — Navya — Anyeshka**

Alicia's post-exile landing spot. She ranks Navya and Anyeshka as her tied #1s. This is a genuine cross-homeroom, cross-ethnic friendship — notable because it cuts against the broader Chinese–Indian separation.

### D. Indian Girls (Babu-centered)
**Navya — Anyeshka** (+ others TBD)

Likely includes Anjolie, Kaisha, Mallorie, and possibly others. Babu homeroom is the center of gravity. Both Navya and Anjolie reference "the Indian girls" as a collective in their rankings, but we haven't pinned down the full membership.

### E. Madeline–Sanvi–Alicia (cross-homeroom)
**Madeline H — Sanvi — Alicia**

Madeline (Johnson) and Sanvi (Clendenon) were an existing pair; Alicia joined recently after her falling out. Madeline ranks Sanvi #1 and Alicia #2 ("only recently"). Madeline and Ada are ex-friends.

### F. Navya–Kaisha (Babu)
**Navya — Kaisha**

A pairing from the far-right circle on the diagram. Ada reportedly admires Kaisha. This may be a sub-unit within the broader Indian girls group rather than a standalone clique.

## 3. Key Dynamics

### The Alicia Situation
The most active conflict. Alicia alleges Mia and Ada are "stealing friends and telling secrets." She was expelled from the Core Four but retains ties to Natalia and Olivia N (both still rank her or are ranked by her at tier 2). She has rebuilt across three different groups: Navya/Anyeshka, Madeline/Sanvi, and her residual Bell friendships. She is the most structurally interesting girl in the network — a bridge across homerooms and ethnic lines — but this position appears to be a product of displacement rather than choice.

**Snack detail:** Alicia reportedly won't share her snacks (strawberry covered pretzels, cheeto fries, chips) with certain girls. This came up unprompted in the interview, suggesting it carries social meaning — either as evidence of the rift or as a grievance in its own right.

### The Ethnic Partition
Chinese and Indian girls "don't really care for each other." This is pervasive enough that the consultant marked it with stars on the diagram. The separation tracks homeroom lines (Johnson = Chinese, Babu = Indian) but persists even in mixed settings. **Two exceptions:** Mia (Chinese, but cordial with Indian girls) and Ada (friends with Anjolie specifically, friendly with Indian girls generally). Both exceptions come from Bell — the "mixed" homeroom with the "nicest teacher."

### Asymmetric Friendships
Several rankings are notably non-reciprocal:
- **Natalia** ranks Mallorie #1, but Mallorie hasn't been interviewed. Natalia does not rank any Core Four member above #3.
- **Olivia N** ranks Kiara (Clendenon) as her #1 — an out-of-homeroom, out-of-clique pick. Her Core Four membership may be more structural than felt.
- **Ada** ties Anjolie with Mia at #1. Anjolie ranks Ada only #2, behind Leela.
- **Anyeshka** ranks Alicia #1. Alicia ranks Anyeshka tied-#1 back — the strongest mutual bond in the dataset.

### Boys (Peripheral)
The girls maintain a boys' popularity ladder: Wade (#1, teacher's pet), Ari (#2), Ethan (#3). Separately, Barack and Sid are "weird boys." There is a running subplot about whether Barack has a crush on Jane — Natalia told Jane she saw Barack reading Babysitter's Club. The girls debate whether attention from weird boys is better than no attention.
