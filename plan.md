## Part 2: New Section 5 — Analysis of Social Mechanisms

Replace the current Section 5 (Follow-Up Questions) with a new Section 5 (Analysis) and move Follow-Up Questions to Section 6.

### Structure of the Analysis Section:

**5.1 The Social Economy: Exchange and Entry**
- "Share secrets, share snacks" as constitutional principle — the group has explicit founding rules
- Snack-sharing as reciprocity ritual (the trash-can "payment" method; covert sharing as bonding through rule-breaking)
- Entry criteria vs. expulsion: Ada frames Alicia's exclusion as failure to meet entry standards, not as active removal
- Food as identity marker: Ada's arroz con pollo association with Natalia; shared eating as belonging signal
- Information as currency: knowing things about boys (Mia's asset), knowing things about Alicia (Lou's asset), knowing things about conflict (Ashley's bridge role)

**5.2 Intelligence and Reconnaissance**
- Proximity testing: two documented behavioral tests (tag test, gossip-detection test)
- Lou as intelligence asset: former Alicia best friend providing insider knowledge; Ada's instrumental framing ("useful to have")
- Mia's cross-gender intelligence: "brave enough to talk to boys" framed as a skill contribution
- Ashley as information bridge: Chinese girls group member who tracks the Alicia/Madeline conflict
- Social shield strategy: using legitimate friendships (Kaisha) as pretext for accessing contested space

**5.3 Conflict Without Confrontation**
- Proxy confrontation: Madeline confronts on Alicia's behalf; Lou runs recon on Ada's behalf — both sides outsource the hard part
- Deniable aggression: head-topping as a game with plausible deniability that becomes proxy conflict
- Quiet disengagement: Ada's signature — she doesn't fight Alicia or Madeline, she just leaves
- Contextual deflection: Alicia's raw onion gambit — creating a social event to escape being cornered
- Performative humor: Ada's "bride and groom may kiss" technique for minor disputes
- Innocence framing: Ada narrates strategic moves but presents each as accidental ("just playing tag")

**5.4 Mediation and Brokerage**
- Two models: Natalia's old "telephone" model (passive relay) vs. Kaisha's active mediation (collects from both sides, stays neutral)
- Mediation failure: "Alicia usually doesn't understand" — Ada attributes breakdown to the recipient, not the method
- Instrumentalization risk: Kaisha's neutral position is exploited by Ada (social shield) and potentially by Alicia's side too
- The mediator's dilemma: Kaisha is valued precisely because she doesn't take sides, but both sides try to pull her onto theirs

**5.5 Displacement and Accumulation**
- The Alicia pattern: girl gets expelled/alienated from a group → bonds with Alicia. Alice (from Lyla), and potentially others
- The Lou pattern: gradual exclusion ("discluded a little") → organic absorption into another group
- These are two distinct displacement mechanisms: rupture (Alicia) vs. drift (Lou)
- Alicia as structural bridge: her cross-homeroom, cross-ethnic network exists precisely because she accumulates displaced friends from different worlds

**5.6 The Ethnic Partition (and Its Limits)**
- Chinese and Indian girls "don't really care for each other" — but the partition is leakier than it looks
- Indian girls absorb non-Indian members (Lou, Maperi) while Chinese girls appear more closed
- Language as boundary maintenance: Chinese girls speak Chinese when together, creating inaccessible in-group moments
- Bridge figures: Mia (Chinese, but Core Five), Ada (half Vietnamese, friends with Anjolie), Lyla (best friends with Anjolie)
- The partition may describe centers of gravity rather than hard boundaries

**5.7 Structural Holes and Scheduling Effects**
- Homeroom as primary sorting variable — but the most interesting social action happens across homeroom lines
- Lunchtime seating (Bell between Babu and Johnson) creates adjacency that enables bridge-building and spying
- Talented-and-gifted scheduling creates Natalia-Ada bonding time (they're the ones left behind)
- Out-of-clique #1 picks (Olivia N → Kiara, Natalia → Mallorie) suggest structural membership can diverge from felt membership
- Recess as primary arena: all documented conflict episodes happen at recess, where homeroom constraints dissolve

**5.8 The Emerging Two-Bloc Structure**
- Core Five (Ada/Mia/Natalia/Olivia N/Lou) vs. Alicia's expanding coalition (Alicia/Madeline/Anyeshka/Navya)
- Madeline on monkey bars with Anyeshka and Navya = cross-homeroom, cross-ethnic alliance signal
- Kaisha as swing figure: genuine mediator being pulled by both sides
- Ada's threat assessment ("they might have more allies, but we have more brains") reveals she sees this as strategic contest
- Boys as peripheral: the girls' social world is almost entirely self-contained; boys are classified and ranked but not integrated
